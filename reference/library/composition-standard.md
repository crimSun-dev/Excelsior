# Manga Composition Standard v2 (learned from the reference library)

Generated by mangaforge from 11,694 usable single pages (series-balanced to 3,229, at most 700 per series), 7 series, plus 227 Link Click scroll strips. Tiers: CRAFT = baseline the checker enforces · TECHNIQUE = proven option · SIGNATURE = one artist's style, study only · K-NITY = house layer, wins conflicts.

## What the reference library says

I ingested **every chapter file in your library**: 788 CBZ files across seven series (Dragon Ball colour, Dragon Ball Super, Hanako-kun, KonoSuba, Link Click, Persona 3 and Persona 5). They hold 741 distinct chapters, because some chapters appear twice from different scanlation groups, and 14,872 pages. 11,694 of those are usable single pages, plus 227 vertical-scroll strips. Every page was measured by machine, and I read 136 of them by eye in this pass, one contact sheet per ingest batch, on top of about 225 read earlier (the 150 most dynamic pages and the 75 tagged reference pages). The notes are in EYE_READING_NOTES.md. Dragon Ball alone is more than half the pages, so the house numbers below are **series-balanced**: at most 700 pages from each series (3,229 pages in all), so no single artist sets the standard. Only numbers and wireframes are stored, never the artwork.

The rules come in four tiers. [CRAFT] rules are near-universal mechanics, and the checker enforces them. [TECHNIQUE] entries are proven tools that you choose to use. [SIGNATURE] entries belong to one artist, so I recorded them for study and did not make them rules. [K-NITY] entries are your house style, and they win every conflict.

## New: every page now borrows from a real page

Instead of building every layout from averages, mangaforge now keeps **7,885 reference pages as layout templates**: their panel shapes, slants, bleeds and insets, cleaned and checked. For each new page it draws a random handful of reference pages with the same panel count and a similar energy, prefers ones whose biggest panel lands on your heaviest beat, spreads the draws across series, adapts each to the K-nity trim, and keeps the best three. Every layout says which page inspired it. Japanese right-to-left pages are mirrored for left-to-right reading. Re-planned this way, Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 reach a median size contrast of about 4.0× and 4.5×, against 1.5–1.8× in v2.

*Pages the detector gets wrong (merged panels, missed panels, balloons caught as panels, afterwords and ads) are filtered by shape checks and by hand as they are drawn. Single-panel pages still come from the engine, because reference splash pages are often several panels merged.*

## The gap this closes

The Chapter 1 and 2 v2 layouts used a median of **3 panels per page**, and the library uses **4**. Our biggest panel was only **1.5–1.8×** the smallest, against a library median of **5.1×** (IQR 2.9–11.1×). Our pages had **no slants and no frame-breaks**, while 23% of library pages slant and 63% bridge panels with art or balloons. That is why the pages felt rigid.

## Craft rules

**[CRAFT] Four panels is the centre of gravity**  
Plan around 4 panels per page. Use 3–6 for most pages, 1–2 only for reveals, and 7+ only for rapid exchanges.  
*Evidence:* Median 4, IQR 3–5, p90 6. Dragon Ball Super runs densest (median 7). Hanako-kun, KonoSuba and Persona 3 sit at 4.

**[CRAFT] One panel must dominate**  
Make the largest panel about 4–5× the smallest. A page of equal boxes reads as a storyboard, not manga.  
*Evidence:* Size contrast median 5.1×, IQR 2.9–11.1×. Hanako-kun pushes hardest (8.3×), Dragon Ball colour is the most even (3.4×). The median panel is 12% of the page and the p90 panel is 41%.

**[CRAFT] Split unevenly**  
When two panels share a row, split it about 60/40, not 50/50. The wider side goes to the beat with more weight.  
*Evidence:* Learned median split across two-panel rows: 0.62/0.38. Neither side is favoured for the wider panel (52/48), so story weight decides.

**[CRAFT] Squeeze the middle tier**  
On three-tier pages, make the middle tier a narrow 'breath strip' (about a quarter of the height) between two fuller tiers.  
*Evidence:* Middle-tier median height is 0.24–0.30 of the page against 0.31–0.43 for the outer tiers, in every common three-tier structure (2|1|2, 1|1|2, 2|1|3, 1|2|1, 2|1|1, 1|2|3).

**[CRAFT] End heavy**  
When a page ends on a hook or an impact, give the bottom tier the most height. The page turn lands on the biggest image.  
*Evidence:* Bottom tier is tallest in 1|2|3 (0.41), 1|1|3 (0.39) and 2|1|3 (0.39). The engine adds 12% to the last tier on impact and reveal beats.

**[CRAFT] Thin gutters, wider between tiers**  
Keep gutters thin, about 0.09 in on a 7 in page. Make the gaps between tiers wider than the gaps between panels in a row.  
*Evidence:* Gutter median 1.35% of page width (IQR 0.82–1.95%). The engine uses 1.45× between tiers and 0.75× within a row, widening on calm and pause beats.

**[CRAFT] Change density every page or two**  
Vary the panel count from page to page. Don't run more than about five pages at the same density.  
*Evidence:* Across 721 chapters, the longest same-density run is median 4 pages, p90 8. Page-to-page change in panel count: median 1, p75 3.

**[CRAFT] Open big, close big**  
Spend splash pages at the chapter's start and end. Keep the middle lean, with one or two big moments.  
*Evidence:* Single-panel and spread pages by chapter tenth: 537, 102, 121, 97, 97, 94, 126, 116, 132, 198. The first tenth holds about a third of them and the last tenth is the next highest.

## Shot language (from 75 reference pages tagged by eye)

**[TECHNIQUE] Mostly medium, punctuated by wides and close-ups**  
Build pages on medium shots and medium close-ups, use wide and full-figure shots to place people in the space, and spend close-ups and extreme close-ups on reactions.  
*Evidence:* Of 239 tagged panels: medium 27%, wide 20%, medium close-up 17%, full-figure 14%, close-up 11%, insert 6%, extreme wide 3%, extreme close-up 2%.

**[TECHNIQUE] Big panels get the wide shots**  
Give wide and full-figure shots the biggest panels and keep inserts and medium close-ups small.  
*Evidence:* Median panel size by shot: wide 28% of the page, full-figure 27%, medium 18%, medium close-up 11%, insert 9%.

**[TECHNIQUE] Pull back at the bottom of the page**  
The last panel on a page is most often a wide shot: the camera steps back as the page turns.  
*Evidence:* Last-panel shot: wide 22, medium 16, full-figure 10, medium close-up 9, close-up 9 (of 75 pages).

**[TECHNIQUE] Tilt the camera when the page is loud**  
Keep most panels at eye level. Save dutch angles for high-energy pages and use low and high angles to show power and vulnerability.  
*Evidence:* Eye level 56%, low 15%, high 14%, dutch 10%, over the shoulder 4%. Dutch-angle panels sit on pages with median energy 6.9, eye-level ones on 5.3.

## Techniques

**[TECHNIQUE] Slanted cuts**  
Tilt tier boundaries by about 6° (5–9°, up to 13° at a climax). In fights, alternate the direction of successive cuts (zig-zag) so the eye ricochets down the page.  
*Evidence:* 23% of pages have a clean slant. The rate is 56% in KonoSuba and 42% in Dragon Ball Super. Angle median 6.5°.

**[TECHNIQUE] Frame-breaks and straddling balloons**  
Let a head, fist, weapon or balloon cross a border into the gutter or the next panel. This is the single cheapest way to make a page feel alive.  
*Evidence:* 63% of pages have art or lettering bridging panels: 85% in Dragon Ball Super, 81% in Hanako-kun, 79% in KonoSuba. The colour Dragon Ball edition reads low (15%) partly because colour makes border-crossing art harder to detect.

**[TECHNIQUE] Bleed the heavy panel**  
Run the dominant panel off the trim on loud pages. Keep margins on calm pages, so the contrast itself signals volume.  
*Evidence:* The print-format Dragon Ball pages bleed on 38% of pages and keep a margin of about 5.7% of page width elsewhere. The scan-trimmed series read 95%+, so treat those as an upper bound.

**[TECHNIQUE] Full-height slivers**  
For standoffs and cast moments, use two or three panels side by side running the full page height. These pages score higher on energy than stacked ones.  
*Evidence:* Single-tier '2' and '3' structures have energy medians of 7.8 and 8.3, against 4.2 for '1|1'. They are most common in Persona 3, KonoSuba and Hanako-kun.

**[TECHNIQUE] Insets as seasoning**  
Float a small reaction panel over the corner of a big one, with a white halo. Use it rarely.  
*Evidence:* Only 4% of pages have an inset (7% in Persona 3), which is exactly why they land.

**[TECHNIQUE] Ink balance**  
Aim for about 14% solid black and about 56% white per page. Dark arcs can push black to 25%+.  
*Evidence:* Black ink median 14% (IQR 10–21%). Persona 3 has a dark-dominant page 51% of the time, Hanako-kun 8%, the others under 6%.

**[TECHNIQUE] Scroll pacing (webtoon mode)**  
In vertical scroll, use the gap as the clock. Short gaps (about 0.05× width) suit action, a 0.18× gap is normal, and 0.4–0.6× is a held breath. Mix tiny insert blocks with tall ones.  
*Evidence:* Link Click strips (227): block height median 0.91× width (IQR 0.19–1.57). Gap median 0.18×, with black gaps only 9% of the time.

## Read by eye: how the pages actually work

Numbers show proportions; reading the pages shows intent. These came from reading 136 pages across every series and arc, one contact sheet per ingest batch.

**[TECHNIQUE] Jump scale inside one page**  
Put an extreme-wide shot with tiny figures next to close-ups on the same page. The jump in scale carries the feeling (loneliness, threat, awe) without a word.  
*Evidence:* HNK ch52 p14 (rubble from above, then faces), HNK ch93 p15 (tunnel mouth), DB ch513 p12 (the Earth from space as the emotional beat), DB ch468 p6 (the clue tiny inside a crater wide).

**[TECHNIQUE] Tease small, reveal huge**  
Show a cropped sliver of the threat first (eyes, a grin, a foot), then give the reveal most of the page, usually low-angle and full-figure.  
*Evidence:* DB ch460 p13 (Buu: tiny face crop, then the whole page), DB ch397 p3 (Cell as a full-height column), DB ch95 p14 (robot towering over the kid in one tall panel).

**[TECHNIQUE] Push the camera in, band by band**  
Three stacked bands that go wide, medium, close: each tier moves the camera closer until the page ends on a face.  
*Evidence:* DB ch440 p10 (kick, hit, scream), HNK ch70 p25 (four strips narrowing to one face), HNK ch68 p12 (crowd wide, then the close-up).

**[TECHNIQUE] Many quick cuts, then one big swing**  
In fights, run a cluster of small slanted panels, then release into one large diagonal panel for the blow.  
*Evidence:* HNK ch85 p7 (four small slants, then the sword panel), DB ch81 p11 (blast diagonal across half the page), DB ch373 p6 (fist bleeding off the page).

**[TECHNIQUE] Lettering is part of the picture**  
Balloon shape is voice: round for normal, angular black for menace, giant black balloons when the line outweighs the art. Big words on black can sit beside a face as one image.  
*Evidence:* HNK ch103 p16 (round vs angular black in one panel), HNK ch109 p20 (the threat lettered huge on black), HNK ch77 p30 (giant black balloons over small figures), HNK ch65 p21 ('SUSPICIOUS.' as a reaction card).

**[TECHNIQUE] Empty space is suspense**  
A tiny balloon or a small walking figure inside a mostly empty panel reads as dead air, doubt or dread.  
*Evidence:* HNK ch78 p19 ('MM.' alone in a white panel), HNK ch111 p6 (empty panel, small figure, big doubt balloon).

**[TECHNIQUE] Take the frame away for tender beats**  
The most intimate or charged moment often has no border: hands, petals, a standoff band floating between framed tiers.  
*Evidence:* HNK ch92 p27 (cherry blossom hands, borderless), HNK ch98 p8 (the unframed 'GLARE' band), HNK ch107 p14 (a face overlapping the tiers above and below).

**[TECHNIQUE] Repeat, then break**  
Identical strips build rhythm; the panel that breaks the pattern is the payoff. Sound effects can be the repeated element.  
*Evidence:* DB ch480 p2 (fusion dance strips, then the flash), HNK ch5 p8 ('RIIING' in every panel).

**[TECHNIQUE] Slivers as a slow pan or a trap**  
A row of tall vertical slivers reads like a slow camera pan, or boxes a character in between two others.  
*Evidence:* HNK ch110 p27 (four slivers, the last one black with narration), DB ch410 p14 (Cell falling between two Gohans), DB ch435 p1 (triptych: foot, leap, face).

**[TECHNIQUE] Chapter-end hook = big image + small wrong detail**  
Last pages pair a large character image with a smaller unsettling detail or shocked face, then a 'continued' bar and a teaser caption down the margin.  
*Evidence:* HNK ch113 p32 (legs-only invitation), HNK ch114 p26, KSB ch108 p30 (shadowed eyes, then the rival in a low over-the-shoulder wide).

**[TECHNIQUE] Things cross borders, not just bodies**  
Props, pointing hands and laughter cross the frame: a hand reaches in from off-panel, a shout breaks the top edge, footsteps are lettered in the gutter.  
*Evidence:* HNK ch1.3 p3 (hand across the border), HNK ch98 p8 ('POINT!' from off-panel), HNK ch102 p4 ('TMP TMP' in the gutter), HNK ch60 p12.

**[TECHNIQUE] Comedy on a strict grid**  
Gags run on dense, regular grids with the punchline in the last tier; a sudden chibi or flat colour background marks the joke.  
*Evidence:* DB ch481 p10, DB ch76 p11, KSB ch5 p27 (chibi punchline), HNK ch74 p25 (chibi tier over a normal tier).

**[TECHNIQUE] Black paper for memory**  
Flashbacks run on black pages with white-bordered panels; SFX sit on the black outside the frames. (This is already K-nity's 'paper is story' rule.)  
*Evidence:* HNK ch78 p8 and p19 (Red House), HNK ch1.1 p10 (memory panel on a black band).

**[SIGNATURE] Toriyama's calm**  
Dragon Ball keeps quiet and talk pages on a strict rectangular grid and opens each arc on a wide city or landscape shot; the grid makes the diagonal fight pages hit harder.  
*Evidence:* DB ch359 p2, ch423 p1 (Capsule Corp wide), ch456 p1, ch375 p2.

**[SIGNATURE] KonoSuba slants everything**  
KonoSuba slants tier boundaries even in dialogue scenes, so the slant becomes house style rather than an action cue.  
*Evidence:* KSB ch67 p10, ch106 p26, ch127 p19.

## Signatures (study, don't copy)

**[SIGNATURE] Dragon Ball Super**  
Fight pages built from horizontal bands with parallel diagonal cuts, speed-line fills and SFX that span the gutters. Dialogue pages fall back to tight rectangular grids, so the contrast is the point.  
*Evidence:* Seen on Ch 2 pp 5 and 8, Ch 3 p 11, Ch 4 pp 5, 7 and 12, and Ch 9 p 23.

**[SIGNATURE] Persona 5**  
Black-heavy pages, angular cuts and cut-in reaction slivers. On spreads, calligraphic text runs in a vertical column beside the splash.  
*Evidence:* Seen on Ch 3 p 25 (spread), Ch 4 p 7, Ch 6 p 36, Ch 7 p 4 and Ch 8 p 18.

**[SIGNATURE] Persona 3**  
Dark-page atmosphere, scratch-texture energy fields and tall vertical slivers, with SFX lettered vertically down the page.  
*Evidence:* Seen on Ch 5 p 29, Ch 10 p 17, Ch 11 p 39 and Ch 25 pp 20–21.

**[SIGNATURE] Hanako-kun**  
Ornamental borders, dense spot blacks, borderless floating panels and decorative caption frames, with a character bursting out of a splash toward the reader.  
*Evidence:* Seen on Ch 58 p 14, Ch 62 p 9, Ch 86 p 29, Ch 87 p 29 and Ch 91 p 2.

**[SIGNATURE] Dragon Ball (colour)**  
Clean rectangular grids with margins, then one huge diagonal action panel. Sound effects overlap the borders.  
*Evidence:* Seen on Ch 1 p 31, Ch 21 p 10, Ch 193 p 11, Ch 304 p 14 and Ch 448 p 2.

## K-nity house layer (wins every conflict)

**[K-NITY] Paper is story**  
Black paper for 2045, amber for the tower glow, white for the present. Gutters are tinted with the static colour in Chapter 2.  
*Evidence:* From Production Bible v2.

**[K-NITY] Lettering voices**  
Ken's mask balloon is a perfect circle with a straight tail. Kkiri's balloon has a cyan-magenta hairline. Calling cards are angular red-and-black boxes, two per chapter at most. Kkiri is pronoun-free.  
*Evidence:* The compositor supports the mask circle and the hairline through letter styles.

**[K-NITY] Silence is lettered as an empty page**  
The 3.2-second silences and 02:47 use void pages and strip pages with widening gaps. The library's pause pacing (gaps of 0.4–0.6× width) is how they read in scroll mode.  
*Evidence:* Bible v2, plus the scroll pacing technique above.
