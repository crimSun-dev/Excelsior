# Shape

Discover what this chapter or scene must do and how it should read, then return a confirmed staging brief without planning pages.

## Phase 1: Discovery interview

Do not plan pages or choose a visual direction yet.

### Cadence

- Use the structured question tool when available; otherwise ask and stop.
- Ask two or three related questions per round, then wait. One round is the default; add a second only when the answers expose a material gap.
- Do not dump a questionnaire, repeat settled facts, or turn obvious facts into menus. Assert the likely reading and invite correction.
- A sparse prompt requires at least one answer round. A precise prompt may need only a compact confirmation.

### Round 1: purpose, reader and outcome

Choose the two or three questions that most change the result:

- What is this chapter or scene for in the series: what changes for the characters, what does the reader learn?
- Where is the reader when they arrive (after a cliffhanger, after a gap, on chapter one), and what do they want?
- What is the one page the reader should remember, and what must they be dying to know at the last page?
- What is uniquely true here that a neighbouring series could not stage the same way?

### Round 2: material, rhythm and boundaries

Run only for material unresolved decisions:

- What script, beats and dialogue exist, and how final are they? What is the page budget (fixed by the magazine, or free)?
- Where do the turns fall: which reveals need a page turn (print) or a long scroll gap (webtoon)?
- Which scenes are Hook, Follow, Converse or Immerse? Where does the silence go?
- What must remain untouched: lines, reveals, canon, previously published pages?
- Format, reading direction, colour, rating, team capacity.

Never ask for panel counts, gutter widths or art-style lanes. New-work owns visual-world and staging-concept choices.

## Phase 2: Resolve the staging direction

For a new chapter in an open world, a new series or a replacement world, follow [new-work.md](new-work.md) through visual authority, any world workshop and the concept roll. Reuse discovery, then return before its contract, persistence or planning. Inside an established world, use its concept process only when the staging remains materially open.

## Phase 3: Write the brief

Write the smallest useful brief to `.excelsior/briefs/<chapter>.md` only after confirmation:

1. **Job and reader:** who arrives, from where, wanting what; the chapter's job in the series.
2. **Outcome and proof:** what the reader understands and feels at the end; the memorable page; the hook.
3. **Selected direction:** visual authority, staging thesis, the chapter's one device, mode per scene.
4. **Page budget and turns:** total pages; a page-by-page beat line (one line per page: beat, energy 0–10, what the turn reveals); splash budget (open big, close big, one or two in the middle).
5. **Scope and boundaries:** what is untouched; explicit anti-goals.
6. **Lettering load:** the talkiest scenes and how they will breathe (talk tiers, reaction pages).
7. **Constraints and open decisions:** format, reading, rating, capacity, and choices the planner must not invent.

Use three to five bullets when the task is settled (a single scene); use the full structure for a chapter.

## Confirm and stop

Present the brief for explicit confirmation or one correction round, then stop: shape never writes beats JSON, plans, or a direction contract.

When no human or structured answer mechanism exists, mark assumptions plainly, return the brief, and stop.
