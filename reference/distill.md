Distill strips a scene or chapter to its essence. Manga is an art of omission: the panel you cut is often the one the reader fills in better themselves.

## Find the fat

- **Redundant beats:** the same information staged twice (she sees it, then we see her seeing it, then she says she saw it).
- **Captions that restate the picture.** Delete; the picture carries it.
- **Transitional panels** that move a character from A to B with no story in the move; cut on action instead.
- **Splashes on non-events** (`slop-splash-inflation`, `craft-middle-splash`).
- **Over-explained reactions:** a reaction shot plus a line saying how they feel.
- **Talk that could be a look.** Converse scenes where a silent medium shot says the line better.

## Cut, then re-pace

- Remove or merge panels; re-plan affected pages with `inspire_page.py` so the remaining beats get real dominance.
- Turn three thin pages into two strong ones and spend the saved page on silence before the peak, or give it back to the page budget.
- Prefer cutting words over cutting pictures; prefer cutting pictures over cutting beats. Never cut a line, beat or reveal the script owns without asking.

## The test

Read the distilled scene once, fast. If nothing is missing and the peak hits harder, it worked. If the reader now has to guess what happened, put back exactly the one panel that answers it.

## Before you finish

List what was cut (pages, panels, words) and what the saved space was spent on. Hand off to `polish`.
