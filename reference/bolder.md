> **Additional context needed**: which page or scene is the target, and what must stay untouched.

"Bolder" is an amplification request, almost always scoped to something that already exists. The surrounding chapter, its grammar and its canon are the given. Your job is to raise one page or scene to the conviction the rest of the chapter already implies, without re-staging anything the brief did not name. The reflex answer, reaching for more speed lines, more tilt and more bleed everywhere, is the opposite of bold; reject it first.

## Scope is sovereign

"Everything else stays" is a literal instruction. Touch only the named pages. Do not restage their neighbours, do not migrate the chapter to a new grammar, do not add paper colours, border styles or lettering voices the series does not already own. If the existing grammar genuinely cannot express it, stop and ask, naming the exact addition and the job it would do.

## Why it reads flat

A page usually reads flat for reasons its neighbours have already solved, or for one of the library's measured reasons:

- **No dominance.** Equal boxes; the hit is not the biggest frame (`craft-equal-boxes`, `craft-peak-not-biggest`).
- **Loud page, quiet geometry.** Energy 7+ with no slant, bleed or frame-break (`craft-flat-loud`).
- **One scale.** Every panel the same shot size; no jump in scale (`shot-monotone-page`).
- **Eye-level everything.** No low angle for power, no high angle for the fall.
- **The turn wasted.** The reveal visible before the page turns.

## The amplification

- **Amplify what the series already owns.** Its signature device, its strongest paper, its heaviest black, its own shout balloon: at full strength, here. The bolder page should look more like the same series, not less.
- **One decisive move, completely.** Pick one of the library's techniques and commit: *quick cuts, then one big swing* (a cluster of small slanted panels releasing into one large diagonal); *tease small, reveal huge* (a cropped sliver, then most of the page, low-angle, full figure); *jump scale* (an extreme wide with tiny figures beside a close-up); *things cross borders* (the fist, the shout, the blade breaks the frame). Then quiet everything around it so the move is legible. If every panel got louder, the page got flatter.
- **Give it its own rhythm.** The bolder page should be a peak in the chapter: a shift in density from the pages either side (fewer, bigger panels after a dense page; a burst of slivers after a calm one).
- **Keep story true.** Lines, beats and canon stay; if the move needs a beat the script does not have, ask.

Re-plan the page with `inspire_page.py PLAN --page N --enforce-peak` (and `--series` to pull from the library grammar that fits: KSB/DBS for slanted bands, DB for the grid-and-diagonal, HNK for extreme contrast and float), then restage shots and angles in the beats.

## The skeleton test

Strip every balloon and SFX from the planned page and look at the bare geometry and shots. Does the skeleton alone say this is the big moment? If it only works once the words return, the boldness is in the lettering size, not the staging.

## Before you finish

- Everything outside the named pages is unchanged.
- No new paper, border, voice or device appeared without being asked for.
- The page still reads in one order and the beat still lands on the turn it was planned for.
- The page is unmistakably the same series, only more sure of itself.
