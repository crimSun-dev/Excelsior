# Craft floor

Load this after the direction is settled, and stage without announcing the checklist. A pinned brief, the series' STYLE.md or the committed visual world overrides anything here; your own habit does not. When the detector hook is active it already enforces the mechanical checks below as you edit plans: act on its findings instead of re-auditing each rule.

## Verify

Each of these is a check on the planned result, not an intention. Run them together in the batched inspection rounds (one proof sheet plus one detector run), not as separate trips.

- **Dominance:** on every multi-panel page one panel dominates, about 4–5× the smallest (library median 5.1×, p25 2.9×). The heaviest beat owns the biggest frame; on impact and reveal pages that is not negotiable.
- **Density:** about 4 panels per page (3–6 for most pages; 1–2 only for reveals; 7+ only for rapid exchanges). Change density every page or two; never more than about five pages at one density.
- **Tiers and gutters:** two panels in a row split about 60/40, never 50/50. On three-tier pages squeeze the middle tier into a breath strip. Gutters thin (about 1.35% of page width), wider between tiers than within a row. End heavy when the page ends on a hook or a hit.
- **Shot language:** build on medium shots (about a quarter of panels), place people with wides and full figures, spend close-ups on reactions, keep inserts for props. Big panels get the wide shots. Open every scene by placing the reader (or tease small and pay the wide off on the same page). No more than four talking heads in a row.
- **Camera:** most panels at eye level; low and high angles carry power and vulnerability; dutch only on loud pages; screen direction holds across a cut unless the cut is the point.
- **Page turns:** in print, reveals land on the page after a turn (the series' `turn_reveals` parity); the page before the turn ends on the tease. In scroll, the reveal sits below a long gap.
- **Lettering:** at most about 25 words per balloon, 40 per panel, three balloons per panel; balloons read in one order (right-to-left series: right to left, top to bottom); no balloon crosses a face or the action's line; small panels hold a word or a reaction, not a speech. SFX are drawn into the picture, not typed on top.
- **Ink and paper:** a deliberate black/white balance per page (library median about 14% solid black, 56% white); the paper colour means something the reader can learn (time, memory, place).
- **Coverage:** every beat, line and reveal in the script is present and findable, in order; every planned panel has a shot size, angle, subject placement and lettering zone.

## Refuse

These are the category's defaults, not bans: the brief's own words can earn any of them. Reaching for one when the axis is free means you were not deciding; recognising that means re-staging the page, not softening it.

Page scaffolds:

- The storyboard grid: equal boxes in rows as the page structure. Equal boxes are the lazy container; a page of them reads as a shot list, not manga.
- The face parade: close-up after close-up of talking heads because faces are easy to prompt.
- The splash inflation: single-panel pages sprinkled through the middle of a chapter because each beat felt important. Splashes are spent at the start and the end; the middle gets one or two.
- A recap caption box or a "Meanwhile…" label opening a scene. This one is a ban, not a default: no brief earns it back. An establishing shot carries place and time; delete the label and let the picture speak. (A series whose canon letters a clock or a date as a designed device, recorded in STYLE.md, is a device, not a label.)
- Panel numbers or reading-order arrows on the page unless the format itself is a puzzle the reader must solve.

Surface habits:

- Speed lines and impact bursts as decoration on pages where nothing moves. Motion comes from staging and cuts first.
- Dutch angles on every panel of an action page. A tilt everywhere is a tilt nowhere.
- Bleed on every page. Bleed is volume; a margin on calm pages is what lets the loud page bleed.
- Everything dead centre: subjects centred in every panel, so balloons have nowhere to sit and the eye has nowhere to travel.
- Screentone gradients as mood wallpaper rather than a value plan.
- A typed comic font for every voice and every SFX. Lettering is voice: balloon shape, weight and face change with who is speaking and how; SFX are drawn in the scene's own hand.
- Generic manga clichés as character acting: the sweat drop, the nosebleed, the vein-pop, the sparkle background, when the series has not chosen them as its comedy grammar.
- Chibi or super-deformed asides in a series that has not established them.
- Light or dark paper picked by genre. Pick it from the scene: when, where, under what light, and what the series' paper rule says it means.

The floor holds the mechanics; it never picks the direction. With every check green, spend the chapter on the committed world, and when torn between tidy and committed, commit.
