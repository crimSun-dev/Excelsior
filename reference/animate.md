Animate is motion in still pages: how the reader feels speed, impact and time, and, for webtoon or motion editions, how the scroll or the screen moves.

## Motion is staging first

- **Cuts carry speed.** Many quick small panels, then one big swing. Density is tempo.
- **Slants carry direction.** Tier boundaries tilted 5–9° (up to 13° at a climax); alternate directions in a fight so the eye ricochets.
- **Things cross borders.** A fist, a blade, a shout, footsteps in the gutter: motion that refuses the frame.
- **Screen direction holds.** Left-to-right movement in a left-to-right book reads as progress; against the reading direction reads as resistance or retreat. Use it on purpose.
- **Multi-image and stroboscopic figures** for a single motion broken into phases; **motion blur and speed lines** for the swing only, never as wallpaper.
- **Time in the gutter.** Wider gutters and an empty panel slow time; overlapping panels speed it.

## Webtoon and scroll motion

The gap is the clock: about 0.05× width for action, 0.18× normal, 0.4–0.6× a held breath (Link Click strips: gap median 0.18×, black gaps only 9% of the time). Tall blocks pull the thumb; tiny inserts snap. A reveal sits below the longest gap. See [webtoon.md](webtoon.md).

## Motion editions

For motion comics or animated covers: one authored move per page (a push-in, a parallax layer, a held breath before the hit), never every panel wiggling; keep the read order intact; let sound follow the lettered SFX.

## Verify

Read the action page fast: can you narrate who moved where, in one pass? If not, `clarify` the geography before adding any more motion.
