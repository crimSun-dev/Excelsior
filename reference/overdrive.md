Overdrive pushes a page or sequence past conventional limits: the set piece readers talk about. Use it once or twice per arc, never per chapter by default.

### Propose before building

Name two or three ambitious stagings for the target, each with what it would cost the art team (pages, redraws, special lettering) and the honest risk. Let the user pick; overdrive is expensive to undo.

## Assess what "extraordinary" means here

- **For Hook:** a reveal the page turn detonates; a double-page spread whose geometry is the reveal.
- **For Follow:** a set piece where the layout itself is the motion (bands that zig-zag down two pages; a single continuous background across a spread with the action stepping through it, handscroll style).
- **For Converse:** a conversation staged as a structure (a nine-panel grid held across pages that breaks once; two faces pushed in band by band until only eyes remain).
- **For Immerse:** a silent sequence; a page where lettering is the image; black paper with a single white line.

## The toolkit

- **Spreads** with nothing vital in the binding gutter; the spread's reading order designed as one page.
- **Panel-less pages:** figures overlapping across the page with no borders, the order carried by gaze and gesture.
- **Meta-layout:** panels shaped by the story (a phone screen, a broadcast frame, cracks in glass, a scoreboard).
- **Lettering as image:** a line that fills the page; a calling card designed as an object.
- **Scale jumps across a turn:** close on an eye at the bottom of the right page; the whole city on the next spread.
- **Rhymes across chapters:** a page that mirrors one from chapter one, panel for panel.

## Discipline

- Reading order stays unambiguous; test with the Dan persona.
- The story beat earns the spectacle; an overdrive page on a non-event is splash inflation with extra steps.
- Production reality: record exactly what the artist must draw differently.
- Print and scroll both: plan how the spread survives `adapt` to webtoon (see [adapt.md](adapt.md)).

## Verify

Proof render, a fast read and a slow read; the critique's heuristics 1, 2 and 10 must not drop.
