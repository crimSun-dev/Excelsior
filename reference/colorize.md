Colorize owns value and colour: the black/white balance of ink pages, screentone, the paper's meaning, colour openers and webtoon palettes. (`tone` is an alias.)

## Value first, even in colour

Plan values per page before choosing tones or hues: where the black sits, where the white rests, what the eye lands on. Library ink balance: about 14% solid black and 56% white per page (IQR 10–21% black); dark arcs push black to 25%+. Persona 3 runs dark-dominant on half its pages; Hanako-kun spots heavy blacks; Dragon Ball keeps pages open.

## Strategies

- **Paper-light:** white pages, black spotted for weight and silhouette. Default for talk-heavy series.
- **Spot-black:** heavy blacks define shapes and night; white is earned.
- **Tone-led:** screentone fields carry mood and time; keep families few (one flat grey family, one gradient, one texture) and consistent.
- **Paper is story:** the paper itself changes to mean time, memory or place (black paper for memory or a future year; amber for a glow; white for now). The reader learns the code; never break it casually.

For colour pages and webtoons: **Restrained** (neutrals plus one accent), **Committed** (one hue carries 30–60%), **Full palette** (3–4 named roles), **Drenched** (the page is the colour). Colour commits at page scale; a hue change is a scene change the reader feels.

## Apply

- Record the plan in STYLE.md (Paper; Tone and value) and the per-page `paper` in the beats.
- Mark value intent in the panel art notes and prompts ("heavy black lower third", "white sky sliver").
- Colour openers: one committed palette, designed for the cover-page moment, consistent with the series' code.
- Check print: tone frequency that will not moiré; rich black only where the printer allows.

## Verify

Proof sheet with paper colours on; page-by-page value rhythm (a dark page earns a light one); every paper change has a story reason.
