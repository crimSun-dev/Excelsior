> **Additional context needed**: which page or scene is too loud, and what feeling it should keep.

"Quieter" calms an overloaded page without killing its energy. Overload is usually loudness everywhere: every panel tilted, every page bled, speed lines on talk, five SFX fighting the art, nine panels of shouting. The reader cannot tell what matters because everything claims to.

## Diagnose the noise

- **Effect inflation:** slants, bleeds, dutch angles and speed lines on calm or talk beats (`craft-slant-calm`, `shot-dutch-calm`, `slop-dutch-spam`, `slop-speedlines`).
- **Density overload:** too many panels for the beat (`craft-overcrowded`), or one density for many pages.
- **Lettering noise:** too many balloons, SFX covering faces, every line a shout balloon.
- **Value noise:** tone everywhere, no white to rest on; black balance far above the series' norm with no dramatic reason.
- **Splash inflation:** big frames spent on non-events, so the real peak has nowhere to go.

## Turn it down, keep the pulse

- Return calm beats to rectangles and margins; keep one slant or bleed for the page's single loud panel, or none.
- Cut density toward the mode's centre (about 4 for Converse, 3–5 for Follow).
- Let one SFX carry the sound; letter the rest smaller or not at all.
- Give the page white: an empty panel, a small figure in a big frame (empty space is suspense), a borderless breath.
- Move a splash's weight back into a multi-panel page and save the big frame for the real peak.

Quiet is not timid: a quiet page should feel deliberate, like the breath before the next hit.

## Before you finish

- The page still has one dominant panel and one clear eye path.
- The scene's feeling survived (tension can be quiet).
- Neighbouring loud pages now read louder by contrast; say which.
