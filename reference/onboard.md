> **Additional context needed**: is this chapter one, a cold open, a new arc, or a return after a hiatus?

Onboard designs how a new or returning reader gets into the story: first chapters, cold opens, cast introductions, world rules, recaps. The aim is the "aha" moment, the page where the reader understands what this series is and wants more, as early as possible.

## The first three pages

- **Page one is the thesis.** Show the series' mechanism in action at the scale it has in the story. Not a logo page, not a recap, not a weather report.
- **Place the reader** within the first spread: one establishing image that answers where and when, unless the mystery of place is the hook (then pay it off before the chapter's midpoint).
- **One character to follow.** Introduce the lead through action before name; let the name arrive in dialogue, not a caption.
- **The first turn pays.** Something the reader did not expect waits on the other side of page one or two.

## Introducing cast and rules

- One new named character per page at most in the opening; a visual tag per character (silhouette, prop, balloon voice) the reader can learn.
- Teach rules by showing a consequence, not by explaining; the explanation, if any, comes after the reader has seen it work.
- Use the series' devices early so the reader learns the code (paper means time; this balloon shape is this voice).

## Returning readers

- A re-orientation beat within the first two pages: a wide of where we are, the lead's face, the open question in one line of dialogue (not a recap box, which the craft floor bans).
- For webtoons, the episode's first screen re-anchors; the last screen hooks.

## Measure

Run the critique with the Dan, Mika and Asha personas. The test: after page three, can a new reader say who this is about, what they want, and what is strange about their world?
